# Attention Wording Review Draft

Status: **DRAFT / REVIEW ONLY**. This document is not bound to a runtime
manifest, does not replace `audio/scripts/attention.yaml` or
`audio/scripts/attention-ocf.yaml`, and is not a production narration master.

Function: `OCF-FND-002` / `PLUS-FOCUS`

## Review decisions

- Keep the preparation, tenth-state, access, focus instruction, sleep-to-twenty,
  return, and closing arc.
- Keep the source-derived concentration, memory, emotional, ease, effectiveness,
  sleep, and waking claims as clearly labelled source-derived claims awaiting
  human content review. They are not OCF efficacy evidence.
- Use a definite inhale-command-exhale sequence without requiring a breath hold
  or offering an optional pause.
- Add one explicit deliberate rehearsal of the focus operation.
- Keep `PLUS-FOCUS` unchanged as the canonical command.
- Use fresh sentence construction rather than copying source wording or merely
  substituting names such as `H-PLUS` and `security repository`.

## Candidate wording

### Opening and preparation

> This is an Open Consciousness exercise for learning focused attention.
>
> Make yourself comfortable in whatever position suits you. Make sure you will
> not be interrupted during this learning period. Settle your body into a
> relaxed position.
>
> Loosen any tight clothing. Stretch your arms and legs, then let them rest.
> Slowly turn your head from side to side. Breathe easily and deeply. I will
> wait while you settle.
>
> Bring to mind the energy conversion box. Open its heavy lid and place inside
> it the worries, anxieties, and concerns you are carrying right now. They are
> not needed for this exercise. Close the lid, turn away from it, and let the
> body settle again.
>
### Tenth-state entry

> Move toward the tenth state of total relaxation as I guide you.
>
> Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine. Ten.
>
> Ten. Rest calmly in the tenth state.
>
### Access opening

> Open your access channel now as I guide you.
>
> Eleven.
>
> Let every part of you, from your waking mind through your whole living system,
> receive and act upon the instruction that follows. Let it become knowledge you
> can use.
>
> Here is the attention instruction.
>
### PLUS-FOCUS teaching pass

> Choose the task that needs your attention. Take an easy breath in and silently
> say `PLUS-FOCUS`.
>
> Bring your attention to the task. Attend to what matters: what you notice,
> what you understand, and what you do next. Keep your thoughts with the work.
>
> Breathe out slowly through your mouth. Continue with the task, noticing its
> details and giving yourself time to take them in. When attention wanders,
> bring it back.
>
> Notice the details you want to remember. Keep them clearly in mind. Let your
> concentration be steady and your manner calm and confident.
>
> Practice this sequence when you need it: choose the task, breathe in, think
> `PLUS-FOCUS`, and breathe out. Build familiarity with the signal through use.
>
### Explicit rehearsal

> Practice now. Choose one sound in the room. Breathe in, silently say
> `PLUS-FOCUS`, and breathe out slowly.
>
> Listen closely for three ordinary breaths. Notice the sound's rhythm and any
> changes. Keep your attention with it. If your attention moves elsewhere,
> return to the sound.
>
> Remember one detail you noticed. Then listen to the room as a whole again.
>
### Access closing

> You can remember and use this learned attention function later. When your
> waking self chooses the action, think `PLUS-FOCUS` and direct attention toward
> the activity you have selected.
>
> The more you use this signal and function, the smoother and easier it will
> become.
>
> Close the access channel now as I guide you.
>
> Ten. Return to the tenth state as the access signal fades. Ten. The access
> channel closes and remains closed until you choose another learning exercise.
>
> Rest calmly in the tenth state.
>
### Sleep induction and twentieth-state repetition

> Move now into easy, normal, restful sleep as I guide you. When the count
> reaches twenty, you will be in normal, natural, restful sleep.
>
> As the number rises, allow relaxation to deepen in whatever way is available
> to you. I will count now.
>
> Ten. Eleven. Twelve. Thirteen. Fourteen. Fifteen. Sixteen. Seventeen.
> Eighteen. Nineteen. Twenty. Twenty.
>
> As you rest, the `PLUS-FOCUS` instruction will repeat quietly, reinforcing the
> attention exercise you have learned.
>
### Return and closing

> I will count from twenty down to one. At one, you will be completely awake and
> rested physically and mentally.
>
> Let attention return to the room, the support beneath you, and the movement
> of your body. Notice sounds and ordinary sensations before opening your eyes.
>
> Twenty. Nineteen. Eighteen. Seventeen. Sixteen. Fifteen. Fourteen.
> Thirteen. Twelve. Eleven. Ten. Nine. Eight. Seven. Six. Five. Four. Three.
> Two. One.
>
> Wake up. Open your eyes when ready.
>
> Your senses will be clear, clean, strong, sharp, and bright. You will wake with
> new energy.
>
> To use the exercise again, choose your task. Breathe in, think `PLUS-FOCUS`,
> and breathe out slowly. Bring your attention to the task and continue.
> This Attention exercise is finished.
>
